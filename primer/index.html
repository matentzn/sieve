    SIEVE in Plain Terms¶

    SIEVE records why Mondo believes a claim, and packages that "why" so a curator, or a machine, can check it.

    A claim is one statement about a Mondo term, for example:

    Down syndrome is not a subclass of hereditary disease

    SIEVE puts that claim, the evidence for and against it, and the decisions taken on it into one evidence packet:

    id: sieve:pkt/MONDO_0008608-excl-MONDO_0003847
type: EvidencePacket
version: "2026-10-20"
statement:
  id: sieve:st/MONDO_0008608-excl-MONDO_0003847
  type: NegativeSubclassStatement
  subject: MONDO:0008608              # Down syndrome
  predicate: {code: rdfs:subClassOf}
  object: MONDO:0003847               # hereditary disease
  negated: true                       # the claim is that it is NOT a subclass
  review_state: VERIFIED
  has_evidence_lines:
    - id: sieve:el/ds-doid-dispute
      type: SieveEvidenceLine
      has_evidence_items: [sieve:ca/doid-14250-isa-630]
      direction_of_evidence_provided: disputes
      score_of_evidence_provided: 0.5
      contributions:
        - id: sieve:act/ds-doid-concordance
          type: InterpretationActivity
          contributor: sieve:agent/concordance-pipeline
          date: "2026-10-20"
          method_type: COMPUTED_CONCORDANCE
          specified_by: [sieve:method/concordance-workflow]
  assertion_decisions:
    - id: sieve:dec/ds-excl-2026
      type: AssertionDecision
      contributor: orcid:0000-0001-5208-3432
      date: "2026-10-20"
      outcome: CONTROVERSIAL
      route: INDIVIDUAL_SIGN_OFF
      inclusion_score: 0.46
      signed_packet_version: "2026-10-20"
prior_claims:
  - id: sieve:ca/doid-14250-isa-630
    type: SubclassStatement           # a prior claim: DOID's own subclass assertion
    subject: DOID:14250               # Disease Ontology: Down syndrome
    predicate: {code: rdfs:subClassOf}
    object: DOID:630                  # genetic disease
    reported_in: [DOID:14250]
    resource: infores:disease-ontology

    Disease Ontology places Down syndrome under its genetic disease class, which grounds to Mondo's hereditary disease, so its assertion enters as a disputing line. A curator looked at it and kept the claim, marked controversial. The rest of this page explains the parts.

    The shape of a packet¶

    graph TD P[EvidencePacket
    one claim, versioned] --> S[Statement
    the claim] S --> L1[Evidence line
    supports] S --> L2[Evidence line
    disputes] S --> D[Assertion decisions
    the verdicts] S --> O[Origin
    where the claim came from] L1 -.-> I1[Item: an OMIM record] L2 -.-> I2[Item: a DOID assertion] P --> I1 P --> I2
    • Statement: the one claim. Its type says which kind of claim it is.
    • Evidence item: one piece of information (a database record, another source's assertion, a passage from a paper, a computed score). Items sit in typed lists on the packet (external_records, prior_claims, text_passages, ...).
    • Evidence line: one judgement that some items bear on the claim, in one direction, with some strength. Lines point at items by id.
    • Assertion decision: whether the claim goes into Mondo, who decided, and on which packet version.
    • Origin: where the claim was copied or cloned from. That is not the same as evidence for it.

    Rules, policies, agents and information resources are shared, so they are maintained once in a registry. Each packet still stands on its own: it carries a frozen reference_snapshot of every registry entry it refers to, as of its version.

    Ten kinds of claim¶

    Each kind of Mondo claim is its own statement class, with its own allowed predicates and its own allowed kinds of evidence:

    ID Claim type Example
    C001 TermExistenceStatement the term exists, is obsolete, is replaced by another
    C002 DefinitionStatement the term has this definition
    C003 SynonymStatement "SPOAN syndrome" is an exact synonym
    C004 SubclassStatement asthma is a respiratory system disorder
    C005 NegativeSubclassStatement Down syndrome is not a hereditary disease
    C006 MappingStatement MONDO:0008608 skos:exactMatch ICD10CM:Q90
    C007 DiseaseAttributeStatement cystic fibrosis has material basis in CFTR
    C008 SubsetMembershipStatement the term is in the GARD rare subset
    C009 MetaclassStatement the term is a grouping class (candidate)
    C010 ValueSetMembershipStatement the term is an included title of an OMIM record (candidate)

    A synonym claim cannot use skos:exactMatch as its predicate, and a definition cannot cite a computed score as evidence. These rules are checked.

    Two levels of judgement¶

    SIEVE keeps two questions apart:

    1. Does this evidence bear on the claim? That is an evidence line: direction (supports or disputes), strength on a 0-1 scale, and who interpreted it and how (curator, panel, pipeline or AI agent). Lines are never edited. A wrong line is invalidated and replaced, and both stay on record.
    2. Does the claim go into Mondo? That is an assertion decision: outcome (ACCEPTED, REJECTED, CONTROVERSIAL, DEFERRED), the route (individual sign-off, consensus, batch sign-off, or an automated policy that a human approved), and the packet version and score it was given on.

    Automated routes can only accept. Anything else goes to a person.

    Three things that are easy to confuse¶

    Axis Question Where it lives
    Provenance Where did this come from, and who did what? origin, contributions, resolution chains, retrieval metadata
    Evidence Does it support or dispute the claim, and how strongly? line direction and strength
    Data quality Was the evidence read and linked correctly? line and step confidence, with its basis

    "This synonym was copied from OMIM in 2019" is provenance. "OMIM still lists it in the 2026 release" is evidence. "The OMIM id was matched to the Mondo term with certainty 1.0" is data quality. A low confidence reduces what a line counts for. It never changes the line's strength.

    From evidence to a score¶

    The inclusion score S is one number between -1 and +1 saying whether the claim should be in Mondo. Each claim type has a versioned aggregation rule that computes it:

    • each line counts its strength, signed by direction, weighted by the quality of the source and by the confidence it was read correctly;
    • lines that echo the same upstream source form one cluster, and only the strongest line in a cluster counts;
    • the sum is added to a prior for the claim type and proposing channel, and squashed into [-1, 1].

    In the published worked examples, cystic fibrosis to CFTR (OMIM, an AI-extracted paper passage and a ClinGen panel) comes out at S ≈ 0.97, and the Down syndrome claim above, with DOID disputing and a curator supporting, at S ≈ 0.46. The score drives the review queue. A person signs it off, and the decision records the S it was given on.

    The scoring code for this model is still being written, so the scores in the example packets are illustrative.

    Checking a packet¶

    uv run linkml-validate -s schema/sieve.yaml -C EvidencePacket my_packet.yaml

    then the profile checks in Python (sieve.profile_checks.check_packet). The Workflows page has the details. The review app, ingest and RDF export are being moved to this model and do not work with it yet.

    Where this comes from¶

    SIEVE is a profile of the SEPIO core information model (Scientific Evidence and Provenance Information Ontology). The core is imported unchanged, and every SIEVE class specialises a core class, so a SIEVE packet is SEPIO data. The profile was built from Mondo's requirements collection; the Requirements Traceability page shows which part of the model answers which requirement.

    For every field, see the Data Model.
